Systems · Growth

Growth Curve updated 2026-11-04

The level cap is 60. Each level takes a fixed ratio longer than the one before — put time on the horizontal axis and your level traces a logarithmic curve (y = k·log x). Early levels come fast, progress slows through the middle, and near the cap a single level takes a very long time (saturation).

  • L1–15 — introduction. Kept as designed for the first hour: about 40 minutes.
  • L15–60 — one ratio. Time to the next level grows by ×1.21 every level. No kinks.
  • End of the main story (L36) at about 22 h total · L60 at about 2,000 h. The last level (L59→60) alone is about 17% of the total.
  • Times are measured with autoplay (the twin). Human play time may differ.

EXP needed

The EXP needed for the next level also follows a logarithmic growth curve — L1–15 is a short introduction, from L16 the requirement grows smoothly, and a second climb starts in the mid-40s, so each level takes longer as you approach the cap. The table only ever moves you up one level at a time (one reward never skips two levels).

  • Quests matter less as you level. Early on quests carry most of your growth; later, repeatable activities — combat training, transit encounters, contested-zone waves and challenges — take over.
  • EXP reward multiplier — every EXP reward is multiplied by a factor based on your level when you receive it: a little lower in the intro band (0.85), 1 around L20, and rising with the requirement at high levels.
  • Quest rewards only get the multiplier up to the quest's own level. Saving quests to finish at a higher level does not make them pay more.
  • Level gap — combat EXP from battlefields far below your level is reduced (Combat · Conflict).
