Systems · Economy

Economy

The galaxy economy does not run in real time; it runs on a once-a-day batch at 12:00 KST. Trades always happen instantly, but their effects reach planet indicators, prices and treasuries only through the batch — the biggest reason this economy feels like "a galaxy the AI keeps running on its own".

The daily batch — what it does

runArcCoreDailyOpsBatch processes, in order: the resource (R) pass → environment pass → conflict aftermath → war-danger index (WDI) and rebellion → master balance → economic fabric rebalance → three-stage price cap adjustment → transport (convoy) settlement → upkeep collection → fiscal closed loop (military spending) → equilibrium adjustment → PGP (planetary gross product) → deed pricing. If you skip days, it catches up — if two or more days are missing it runs right away on your next login, and if yesterday is already processed it waits until today's 12:00 has passed (prevents early runs just after midnight · adjusted 2026-10-03). This is the actual mechanism behind the feeling that "the world moved while I was away".

Price elasticity = 0 Individual trades are not reflected in prices in real time. Prices move only in the batch's three-stage cap adjustment.

The five planet indicators & PGP

Every planet has five indicators (0–100): Resources (R), Population (P), Defense (D), Technology (T) and Environment (E). Together they produce PGP (planetary gross product):

PGP = (R+P+D+T+E) / 5 × 3,375 — with all five at 50, PGP is 84,375

PGP is recalculated only in the daily batch. Real-time trades cannot move the indicators directly; a day's worth of observations from convoys and player trade volume is applied lightly in the batch, capped at ±2.

The five vaults — the factions' coffers

Galaxy money flows into five independent vaults. They are also treated differently when an account is reset (purged).

Seed values: transport convoys 500,000 cr · ArcCore (RED) 100,000 cr · Blue 100,000 cr. The central bank's surplus is split 34% fleet spending, 33% expansion spending and 33% development budget.

Trade and fees

Seventeen trade ports link supply and demand planets. The trade fee is 10% of the total — 5% goes to that planet's player wallet pool (settled once a day) and 5% goes immediately to the occupying faction's vault. Convoys pay the full cost up front and send 30% of their margin to the central bank, keeping 70% (no fixed costs — the structure is meant to build up a surplus).

Upkeep

Each planet costs a base 800 credits a day, plus level-based upkeep for developed facilities such as defense satellites, drawn from the occupying faction's vault. If the balance runs short it stops at zero and only logs upkeep_shortfall (no penalty yet).

Minerals and mining

The galaxy has two mineral regions: the Core Trade Belt (safety and trade, 35% of galaxy clusters) and the Frontier Rim (neutral and high-risk, 28%). Most of the 21 core planets and the Synth planets belong to one of the two. Mining rewards currently go only to the player's inventory and are not yet linked to a planet's R indicator — a planned follow-up connection in the design.

Fiscal and military closed loop

Since 2026-08-25 the daily batch has run a live fiscal opex pass. Using the D (defense) indicator, PGP, ship count and research/captain levels, it calculates each faction's "military spending" and actually removes credits from its vault — a military-spending proxy that replaced the old "empty burn". It is only an aggregate per faction, though; it does not simulate each individual ship.

A known structural gap Resetting an account (personal purge) and "resetting the world economy for service launch" are different levers, but there is no dedicated API for the latter yet — flagged as the top-priority gap in the 2026-08-04 audit.
