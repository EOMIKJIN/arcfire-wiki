World · Background

Setting

You are a mid-ranking captain posted to the border planet Arcadia while the Crimson Legion, which controls the east of the galaxy, is at war with the Stellium Union in the west. There are no other players, but the galaxy really is run by an AI called ArcCore — in this game the setting is not window dressing; the economy, combat and dialogue systems are built on it.

Core loop

The game moves between three stages.

STAGE 1

Planet Hub

Scan → mine → trade → shipyard → bar and NPC conversations

STAGE 2

Galaxy Map

Travel between systems; encounters can break out along the way

STAGE 3

Combat

Real-time orbital combat · wave defense

Your home planet develops along five indicators — resources, population, defense, technology and environment — and these indicators underpin the entire economy. The game is designed as a non-competitive single-player sandbox: there is no pay-to-win pressure pitting you against other players.

What is ArcCore?

ArcCore is the backend AI that actually runs the galaxy's economy, factions and front lines — and it is also part of the story. The operator Stella Aris, who speaks to you at your side, is a colleague who reads the battlefield and suggests your next move. The details are covered in ArcCore AI.

Setting details

  • The game's title is a place name — "Arcfire" is the planet at the far eastern edge of the galaxy (the Crimson Legion's capital) that holds an eternally burning fire-energy core. All three powers fight over the ancient energy sealed inside it.
  • Two planets for the beginning and the end — "Eternity" (the final-boss planet, where past, present and future overlap), the end point of the galaxy's story, and "Genesis" (with primordial seas and life, the origin of all things) are deliberately designed as a matched pair symbolizing the beginning and the end.
  • Synth colonies — besides the 21 core planets, ArcCore's "automatic colonization protocol" opens up Synth planets over time (76 of the base 79 currently appear on the galaxy map). It is a setting device showing that the galaxy keeps being settled even while you play.
  • Perseus Memorial — an entire planet turned into a war memorial for the fleets lost in the legendary "Battle of Perseus". It hints at galactic history the story has yet to tell in full.
